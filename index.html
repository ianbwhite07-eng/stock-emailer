VIX — Market Fear Gauge
Measures expected S&P 500 volatility over the next 30 days. Often called the "fear index." Low VIX = calm risk-on market; high VIX = fear, uncertainty, often accompanies sharp selloffs that hurt small/mid-caps more.
<15 — calm, risk-on+1 short score
15–30 — normal volatilityno adjustment
>30 — elevated fear−1 short score
HY Credit Spread (bps)
Extra yield investors demand to hold high-yield ("junk") bonds vs. Treasuries. Wide spreads mean credit markets are stressed — small and mid-cap companies pay more to borrow and face higher default risk. This matters more than any single analyst rating.
<300 bps — tight, risk appetiteno adjustment
300–500 bps — normal rangeno adjustment
>500 bps — credit stress−1 long score
Takes priority over yield curve — both don't stack.
10Y–2Y Spread — Yield Curve
Long-term minus short-term Treasury yield. A positive spread is the normal state (longer loans cost more). When it inverts (goes negative), banks earn less lending long vs. borrowing short — credit tightens, and recessions historically follow within 12–18 months.
>0% — normal slopeno adjustment
−0.5–0% — flatteningno adjustment
<−0.5% — inverted−1 long score*
*Only if HY spread is not already triggering.
Fed Funds Rate
The Federal Reserve's benchmark overnight lending rate. Higher rates raise borrowing costs economy-wide, compress valuations (especially for growth stocks), and tend to pressure small caps that rely on debt. Lower rates = tailwind for equity risk-taking.
